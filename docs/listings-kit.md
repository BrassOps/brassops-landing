## Copy

**Tagline (60 chars)**
Firearms training records that hold up.

**Short (160 chars)**
BrassOps records police firearms qualifications, remedial training and range days as they happen, so command knows who is ready and the agency can prove it.

**Medium (400 chars)**
BrassOps is firearms training record software for police departments. Scores are logged on the firing line from a phone, remedial cases open when a qualification fails, instructor certifications are checked against every session date, and every edit is logged. When a records request or a subpoena arrives, one officer's complete training file is a one-minute packet instead of a week in the filing room.

**Long (listing description)**
BrassOps replaces paper and spreadsheets for a police department's firearms training program. It records qualifications, practice sessions, remedial training, range days, weapons and ammunition in one place, with the standard each session was judged against frozen on the record. Command staff see readiness by weapon type at a glance. Range staff log scores from a phone on the line, even offline. Officers see their own qualifications and sign range-day forms on their phone. Legal and risk get the Records Request Packet: every session in a date range, failed attempts included, the instructor's certification on that date, signed forms and the full change history, ready for a subpoena or OPRA request.

Built by a former range instructor for the departments he trained with. Multi-tenant, each agency's data isolated, two-factor authentication for every user, stored files served through BrassOps rather than public links. Annual pricing per department.

**Key features (bullets for listings)**
- Qualification tracking with the standard frozen on each record
- Mobile score entry on the firing line, offline capable
- Automatic remedial case when a qualification fails
- Range day console: roster check-in, safety checks, relays, after-action report
- Armory: weapons by serial, inspections, repairs, rounds fired, ammunition lots
- Instructor certification tracking checked against every session date
- Records Request Packet for subpoenas, discovery and internal inquiries
- Officer readiness dashboard by weapon type
- Target photo analysis with coaching drills
- Full activity log: who changed what, when

## Where to list, in order
1. **Google Business Profile** (business.google.com): category "Software company", address Allenwood NJ (hide street address, service area New Jersey), hours, website, phone, the short description, 5 photos, one post a month (a blog post works). Verifies by postcard or phone.
2. **Capterra / GetApp / Software Advice** (one submission covers all three, vendors.capterra.com): category Law Enforcement Software, then Public Safety. Free listing; the medium and long copy, 10 features, 5 screenshots. Then ask 3 customers for a review (they email a link; a review with a badge number is credible).
3. **G2** (sell.g2.com): Public Safety and Law Enforcement categories. Needs 1 review to go live.
4. **GovSpend / GovTech vendor directories**, **Police1 Product directory** (police1.com/products), **Officer.com Buyer's Guide**: free listings, law enforcement audience. Use the long copy.
5. **NJ State Association of Chiefs of Police** associate membership (vendor listing + conference access) and **NJ League of Municipalities** exhibitor listing: paid, but they are the rooms the buyers are in.
6. **LinkedIn company page**: already exists or create; add the tagline, the long copy, the five screenshots, and post each blog article.
