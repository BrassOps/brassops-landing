The Hidden Cost

What Paper Records Are Actually Costing You

The real price of paper isn't ink and folders.

Physical Vulnerability

Paper burns. Paper floods. Paper gets lost between desks, misfiled in wrong folders, or thrown out during office cleanups. One incident can destroy years of training documentation with no way to recover it.

Zero backup, zero recovery

Legal Weakness

Paper score sheets with no timestamp verification, no chain of custody, and potentially illegible handwriting are the weakest possible evidence in court. Opposing counsel will question when it was created, by whom, and whether it was altered.

40% higher audit failure rate

Operational Drag

Every minute spent filing, retrieving, cross-referencing, and copying paper records is a minute not spent training officers. The administrative overhead of paper compounds every range day.

15-25+ hrs/month on paperwork

In the Courtroom

Paper Under Cross-Examination

Courts evaluate four things in negligent training cases. Paper records fail most of them.

1
Training Existed
PaperYou have a score sheet. But the date is handwritten. The ink looks different. Was it created at the time of training, or after the subpoena?
BrassOpsDigital record with immutable timestamp. Created at the point of the event. No ambiguity.
2
Training Was Appropriate
PaperA score and a course name. No details about what was covered, what drills were run, or what the instructor observed.
BrassOpsSessions linked to courses of fire, coaching notes, and instructor observations. Complete training context.
3
Officer Received It
PaperA signature on an attendance roster. That proves they signed a piece of paper, not that they fired a weapon.
BrassOpsScore-based attendance verification. The officer was there, they fired, their performance was measured.
4
Officer Showed Competency
PaperA handwritten pass/fail. No supporting data. No diagnostics. No trend analysis.
BrassOpsGranular scores, behavioral analytics, performance trends, and AI-assisted target analysis.
